01 · The Invoice

Design doesn't ask for budget. It shows the cost of its absence.

At Yubi I put a number on design fragmentation and showed the CPTO the invoice. That conversation happened once. Y.Design was funded the next quarter and cut operating costs by ₹72 crore in FY24 and ₹186 crore in FY25.

02 · Sherlock

Diagnose before you design.

Audit everything, name every inconsistency, and let the data make the argument. 500+ screens audited at Yubi became a two-year roadmap nobody could argue with. NPS went from 1.7 to 8.23.

03 · The Trust Architecture

Trust is not a brand attribute. It's a designable system.

In financial products, every screen either builds trust or breaks it. Designing it deliberately is how Aspero went from ₹18 crore in losses to ₹860 million in revenue in 18 months.

04 · The AI-Speed Org

A design org that runs on manual handoffs is already legacy.

Token automation, AI-assisted review, Figma-to-UI acceleration. At Yubi this meant a 2-3x velocity advantage. As a consultant I've cut client design-to-development cycles by up to 40%.

Building organisations

The real deliverable is the organisation.

Screens get redesigned. Systems get replaced. The thing that compounds is an organisation that keeps making better decisions when you're not in the room. That's the test I hold myself to: not the work produced while I'm there, but what keeps being produced after I leave.

The first build · HashedIn · 2015 to 2019

One designer among five hundred engineers.

I joined as the first and only designer at a company that didn't yet believe design mattered. Four years later, HUX was a 32-person practice with its own vision, operating model, hiring strategy, design standards, and client methodology. Not a support function: a revenue-generating vertical serving more than 20 enterprise clients across the US, UK, and India. When Deloitte acquired HashedIn, the practice went with it.

1 to 32 designers · Revenue-generating vertical · Survived the acquisition

The second build · Yubi · 2022 to 2025

Two designers. Five brands. A $140M ecosystem in chaos.

Yubi wasn't about proving design mattered; it was about rebuilding a function that had been outrun by its own company's growth. Working directly with the CEO, CPO, and CTO, I built the 20+ person organisation spanning Associate Design Directors, Design Leads, Product Designers, UX Researchers, and Motion Designers. Leadership grew from within: the people I hired now run the teams. The org kept 94% of its people in a market that was shedding designers.

2 to 20+ across 13 products · Leadership grown from within · 94% retention

The evidence

Both practices outlasted me.

That's the part I'm proudest of. The rituals, the standards, the career ladders, and the culture kept running after I left. An organisation that needs its founder in the room isn't an organisation yet. It's a bottleneck with a title.

How I lead

Culture is the operating system. I take it seriously.

Safety
Psychological safety first. People do their best work when it's safe to be wrong in public. I build teams where critique is a gift, not a threat.
Ownership
Designers own outcomes, not tickets. Every designer on my team can tell you what business metric their work moves, and why it matters.
Craft
Standards that speed you up. Design rituals, quality reviews, OKR-driven planning. Governance done right removes decisions, not freedom.
Growth
Leadership grown from within. I promoted Associate Design Directors and Leads out of my own team. The org chart should be a ladder others climb, not a moat around the person at the top.
Fluency
Teams that speak business. My designers learn to talk revenue and cost, because budgets listen to business cases, not good intentions.
Now

Consulting today. Choosing the next seat deliberately.

Since October 2025 I've worked as an independent AI design consultant, helping CPOs and CTOs embed tools like Claude, Figma AI, and Cursor into their design organisations. Done right, design-to-development cycles drop by up to 40%. The work is sharpening the playbook I'll bring to my next role.

What I'm looking for now: a Director or VP of Design seat at a company where design is meant to be a competitive advantage.
