Dear future CEO,

Somewhere in your company there's a product everyone has quietly given up on. Nobody says it in the roadmap review, but the belief has left the room. I'd like to talk about that one first.

Reconciled · ₹860M revenue

The last time I met a product like that, it was a bond platform losing ₹18 crore a year, with fewer than 300 customers. I treated the better version of it as already real and worked backwards. Eighteen months later it was earning ₹860 million, a third of the company's revenue, and it had become the product everyone pointed to. The strategy wasn't genius. It was belief, with a work ethic.

The pattern · believe first

I should explain the name. In interface engineering, an optimistic update is when the screen shows you success before the server confirms it: the heart fills the instant you tap it, and reality catches up a moment later. That pattern is how I run products, teams, and my own career. Believe first. Then make the server agree.

Worth it

I came to design sideways. I was a developer, a good one, who kept asking a question that wasn't in any brief: does this feel right to the person using it? That question cost me a perfectly fine engineering career. I've never once wanted it back.

1 to 32 · then 2 to 20+

Since then I've built the design function twice, from scratch. At HashedIn I was the first designer among five hundred engineers; four years later there were thirty-two of us, a revenue-generating practice that survived the Deloitte acquisition. At Yubi I inherited two designers and five brands in beautiful, expensive chaos, and built the twenty-person organisation that untangled it, working directly with the CEO, CPO, and CTO. Ninety-four percent of those people stayed, in a market that was shedding designers.

Reconciled · ₹186Cr saved

You should also know how I ask for things, which is that I mostly don't. When Yubi's design chaos needed a system, I didn't request a budget. I priced the chaos and showed the CPTO the invoice. That conversation happened once. The system it funded cut operating costs by ₹72 crore the first year and ₹186 crore the next, numbers confirmed by finance, not by design.

Both outlasted me

And here's the part I'm proudest of, which is also the part that matters most to you: both organisations kept running after I left. The rituals, the standards, the leaders I promoted, still there. An organisation that needs its founder in the room isn't an organisation. It's a bottleneck with a title. I build the other kind.

Design-to-dev · down 40%

Right now I'm between builds, on purpose. I spend my days as an independent consultant teaching design organisations to run at AI speed, with tools like Claude, Figma AI, and Cursor. Done right, design-to-development cycles drop by up to forty percent. Whoever hires me next gets that playbook on day one.

Open · Director / VP of Design

So here's my ask. If you're building a company where design is supposed to be a competitive advantage and isn't yet, that's the seat I'm looking for: Director or VP of Design, in India, the UK, Singapore, the US, or remote. And if you're not sure exactly what you need, even better. Some of my favourite conversations started there.

Your company has a better version too. I can probably already see it.

Optimistically,
Subin Sundaresan
