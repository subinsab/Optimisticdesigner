The diagnosis
Project Sherlock:
500+ screens. Every inconsistency named.

Before fixing anything, we needed to know exactly what was broken. Project Sherlock was a data-backed design audit — heatmaps, analytics, session recordings, and automated screenshot mapping across every Yubi product.

500+ screens audited. Every navigation pattern, filter placement, table layout, and card style catalogued and compared. The finding was stark: without a shared design language, every product team had made rational local decisions that added up to an irrational experience for users moving between products.

The NPS of 1.7 wasn't mysterious. Drop-offs were at 87%. Users were confused, stakeholders were frustrated, and the engineering team was fixing the same UI bugs in six different products simultaneously.

"Project Sherlock didn't discover problems — it named them. Naming a problem precisely is the first step to owning it."

The programme
Standardisation at
enterprise scale.

UNIFY wasn't a redesign. It was a systematic dismantling of four years of fragmentation — and a rebuild from a single source of truth. Working with design leads across all five product brands, we standardised navigation patterns, page layouts, filter interactions, data table structures, and loading states.

We introduced a unified accessibility framework — WCAG AA compliance across all core flows. A new shared design language that felt like an evolution of each product's visual identity, not a takeover. And a governance process that ensured new designs were reviewed against UNIFY standards before shipping.
