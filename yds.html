The problem
44 designers. 13 products.
Zero common language.

As Yubi scaled across five product brands, design fragmentation became a hidden tax on everything. Without a shared system, every team interpreted core patterns in their own way. Buttons had multiple styles. Navigation varied by product. Tables were redesigned from scratch for every new feature.

Every inconsistency was a conversation that hadn't happened. Every rework was a cost nobody had calculated. So I calculated it.

₹136 crore. That's what design fragmentation was costing in engineering rework over three years. I didn't ask for a design system budget. I showed the CPTO the invoice.

"That conversation happened once."
The architecture
Seven layers.
One source of truth.

YDS was built as a layered, modular system scalable across 13+ products and teams. Each layer had a specific job — and together they created a system that could flex for every product context while maintaining brand consistency.

01Base TokensRaw colour, spacing, typography — the atomic foundation. Everything else is built on these.
02Core Semantic TokensIntent-mapped values. Designers think in purpose, not hex codes.
03Theme TokensLight/dark/brand-specific UI kits. Drop into any product context.
04Locale & LocalisationRTL, regional typography, cultural adaptation. Built in from day one.
05Components98 Figma + code-backed components. Buttons to complex data tables.
06Patterns & Workflows6 standardised flows covering ~80% of product use cases.
07Documentation & GovernanceSingle source of truth. Designers and engineers speak the same language.
The AI angle
We were early on AI
because we had to be.

A 20-person design team serving 13 products cannot operate on manual handoffs. We integrated Token Studio for automated token management, Figma automation for component variant generation, and AI-assisted design review workflows that caught inconsistencies before they reached development.

This wasn't a trend play. It was an operational necessity — and it gave us a 2-3x velocity advantage over what was possible with manual processes alone.
