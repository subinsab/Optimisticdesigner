Case study · Y.Design · Yubi · 2022 to 2025

One system. ₹186Cr saved.

Thirteen products across five brands with zero common language. Y.Design, a token-based multi-brand system, unified the $140M ecosystem and cut operating costs by ₹72 crore in FY24, then ₹186 crore in FY25.

01 · The business situation
Fragmentation was a hidden tax on everything.

As Yubi scaled across five brands, every team interpreted core patterns its own way. Buttons had multiple styles. Navigation varied by product. Tables were rebuilt from scratch for every feature. Each choice was locally rational; together they were an invisible cost centre.

Every inconsistency was a conversation that hadn’t happened. Every rework was a cost nobody had calculated. So I calculated it: crores of engineering rework a year, all traceable to fragmentation.

02 · The strategy · The Invoice
I didn’t ask for a budget. I showed the invoice.
Design systems are business systems. Tokens are just how they’re spelled.

I never pitched a design system as a design initiative. I presented the annual cost of not having one, in currency the CFO reads. That conversation happened once, and Y.Design was funded and built starting the next quarter.

The architecture followed the business case: seven layers from base tokens to governance, 98 code-backed components, and six standardised workflow patterns covering roughly 80% of product use cases, all themable across five brands from a single source of truth.

03 · The team, and how I ran it
A platform pod at the centre, contributors at the edges.

A dedicated system pod inside the 20+ person design organisation owned the core. Product designers contributed patterns back through a governance process, so the system stayed close to real product needs instead of becoming an ivory tower. Engineering co-owned the tokens.

And because a 20-person team serving 13 products cannot run on manual handoffs, we automated early: Token Studio pipelines, automated component variant generation, and AI-assisted design review. The front-end footprint needed to maintain UI went from 68 engineers to 12.

Operating costs fell by ₹72 crore in FY24 and ₹186 crore in FY25. UI bugs per quarter dropped from 139 to 4. Adoption climbed from 30% to 85%. And for the first time, thirteen products felt like they were made by one team.

06 · What I carry forward
Make the cost of bad design visible, and the C-suite starts asking for design.

Y.Design proved that the fastest way to fund design maturity is not evangelism. It is accounting. Price the fragmentation, show the invoice, and the organisation will build the system for you.
