Reward by Design · Khatabook · 2021 · Behavioural UX
Turning data entry
into storytelling.

How a boring KYC form for 10 million merchants became an animated, emotional experience — and what that taught me about how design should work.

CompanyKhatabook
My roleLead Designer
Users10M+ merchants across India
ScopeOnboarding UX · Behavioural design · Motion
Outcome+22% activation · Increased completion
The brief
A form that 10 million
merchants had to fill in.

The KYC (Know Your User) flow was one of the most important screens in Khatabook — and one of the most ignored. It collected essential business information: store name, address, staff size, business type. Standard stuff. Boring stuff.

Completion rates were low. Drop-offs were high. The analytics told us users were stopping at fields they didn't feel like filling in — not because the form was hard, but because it felt like paperwork. There was nothing in it for them. No acknowledgement that this information was going to mean something.

The brief was to improve completion. The question I asked first was: why would a merchant care about filling this in?

The insight
We didn't gamify.
We acknowledged effort.

The temptation was to add gamification — points, progress bars, badges. We didn't. Gamification treats users like they need to be tricked into completing something. That's manipulation, not design.

The real insight was simpler: small business owners in India are proud of their businesses. Their shop is their identity. If we could make the product reflect that — show them their shop taking shape as they entered their details — the form wouldn't feel like paperwork. It would feel like building something.

"We turned data entry into storytelling. The merchant wasn't filling in a form — they were opening their shop."

The psychology
Why it worked.

The design was grounded in four behavioural principles that explain why people engage with — or abandon — digital experiences.

Dopamine Response
Reward anticipation triggers motivation. Seeing the shop build up as you fill in details creates a sense of progress and ownership.
Cognitive Ease
Our brain rewards clarity. Design that's easy to process feels right. The visual representation removed cognitive load from the task.
Completion Bias
Progress visuals release satisfaction signals. Seeing something half-built creates a drive to finish it.
Micro-feedback
Even subtle motion says "you did it." Each completed field triggered a small animation — not a celebration, just an acknowledgement.
The outcomes
What changed.
📈Increased completion rates
😊Emotional connection
🪞Users saw themselves in the product
+22%Activation rate improvement

The bigger lesson from this project wasn't the activation number — it was a principle I've carried into every piece of work since: design should reward behaviour, not manipulate it. The difference between the two is respect. Gamification asks "how do we get users to do what we want?" Good design asks "how do we make doing the right thing feel good?"
