Case study · Reward by Design · Khatabook · 2021

Data entry, turned into story.

A KYC form that 10 million merchants had to fill became an animated shop-building experience. Activation rose 22%, and it settled a question I have carried ever since: reward behaviour, never manipulate it.

01 · The business situation
The most important screen was the most ignored.

KYC was one of the most important flows in Khatabook, and one of the least loved. It collected essential business information from shop owners: store name, address, staff size, business type. Standard, boring, necessary.

Completion was low and drop-offs were high, and the analytics said users were stopping at fields they simply didn’t feel like filling. Not because the form was hard. Because it felt like paperwork, with nothing in it for them. The brief said improve completion. The question I asked first was: why would a merchant care?

02 · The strategy · Reward, don’t manipulate
We didn’t gamify. We acknowledged effort.
The merchant wasn’t filling in a form. They were opening their shop.

The obvious play was gamification: points, badges, progress bars. We rejected it. Gamification treats users like they need to be tricked into finishing, and that is manipulation wearing design’s clothes.

The real insight was simpler. Small business owners in India are proud of their shops. The shop is the identity. So we made the product reflect that: as a merchant entered details, their shop visibly took shape on screen. Grounded in four behavioural principles: reward anticipation, cognitive ease, completion bias, and micro-feedback.

03 · The team, and how I ran it
A tight pod, run like a growth experiment.

A small pod: design, product, analytics, and motion. Every choice was a hypothesis with a metric attached, instrumented before it shipped.

The leadership work was mostly restraint: holding the line against stakeholder pressure to add points and badges, and making the case that respect converts better than tricks. The results settled the argument.

04 · The design moves
Four principles, made tangible.
01Reframe the jobNot filling a form. Opening a shop. The mental model changed before any screen did.
02Build progress you can seeThe shop assembles field by field, so effort has a visible, personal payoff.
03Acknowledge, don’t celebrateEach completed field earns a small motion cue. A nod, not fireworks.
04Measure respectfullyActivation and completion, instrumented honestly. No streaks, no dark patterns, no guilt.
05 · Business outcome
An emotional connection to a utility app.
+22%Activation rate improvement
10M+Merchants on the platform
11 fieldsMade worth completing
0Dark patterns shipped

Activation improved 22% and completion rose across the whole KYC flow. The unmeasurable outcome was the more important one: merchants saw themselves in the product. A bookkeeping utility earned an emotional connection, which is the hardest currency in consumer fintech.

06 · What I carry forward
Design should reward behaviour, not manipulate it. The difference is respect.

Gamification asks: how do we get users to do what we want? Good design asks: how do we make doing the right thing feel good? I have applied that distinction to every product since, at every scale.
