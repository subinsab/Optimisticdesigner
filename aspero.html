Case study · Aspero · Yubi · 2022 to 2025

A $1.7M loss, turned into $430M.

There was no retail product when I joined, only a thesis that wasn’t landing. A design-led initiative took it from MVP to tested app to an independent brand my team created: Aspero. From a $1.7M loss in FY23 to $430M in FY25.

01 · The business situation
There was no product. There was a thesis that wasn’t working.

Yubi’s fixed income business had real B2B heritage, serving wealth distributors and financial advisors, and a real market thesis: retail bond investing was one of the most underserved spaces in India. But the retail attempt, then carrying the Yubi Bonds name, wasn’t landing. FY23 closed at a $1.7M loss, and internally the appetite for another try was thin.

This is the moment most companies hand to a product team with a backlog. Yubi handed it to design. Not to redesign an app, because there was no app worth redesigning, but to find out whether a retail fixed income business could exist at all, and what it would have to feel like.

02 · The strategy · The Trust Architecture
MVP first. App later. Belief throughout.
Retail investors don’t buy bonds. They buy certainty.

We refused to build the full product on an unproven thesis. The sequence was deliberate: a lean MVP first, real users on it fast, and every assumption written down as a testable hypothesis. Positioning, audience, ticket sizes, the language of risk, the role of the brand, all of it went through the same loop: test, learn, keep or kill.

The biggest kill was the hardest one: the umbrella brand itself. The hypotheses kept converging on the same finding, retail investors would not extend trust to a name built for institutional lending. The product didn’t need a better interface. It needed its own identity.

03 · The team, and how I ran it
One small pod carried strategy, brand, and product.

Three designers, one researcher, and one content strategist, run as a single unit across what would normally be three departments. My team created the Aspero brand end to end: the name, the identity, the voice, all built around calm and certainty instead of fintech noise. The same pod designed the product, so the brand promise and the product behaviour never diverged.

The operating rhythm was executive by design: weekly reviews with the CEO, CFO, and CPO on hypotheses and business metrics. Design wasn’t presenting screens for approval. Design was reporting on the business it was building.

04 · The turnaround
From thesis to three platforms.
01Ship the MVP before the appA lean MVP put the thesis in front of real investors in weeks, not quarters. Evidence before investment.
02Test hypotheses, kill the wrong onesAudience, positioning, ticket sizes, trust language, every assumption ran the same loop, and the data retired the Yubi Bonds direction.
03Brand it independentMy team created Aspero: name, identity, and voice, engineered for certainty. The rebrand was a trust decision, not a cosmetic one.
04Design all three sidesB2C for retail investors (onboarding as a guided story, KYC down from 11 steps to 5), B2B for wealth partners, and B2B2C distribution rails.
05 · Business outcome
The initiative nobody wanted to fund became the growth story.
$430MFY25, from a $1.7M loss in FY23
20K+Active investors, from under 300

3Platforms: B2C, B2B, B2B2C

FY23 closed at a $1.7M loss under the old direction. FY25 closed at $430M, with 20,000+ active investors on a platform that started with fewer than 300, contributing 32% of Yubi’s revenue along the way. Aspero launched as an independent brand in October 2023 and now stands as its own SEBI-registered platform.

06 · What I carry forward
Products fail on belief before they fail on features.

Aspero settled two convictions. First, design can lead a zero to one, not just polish one, if it is accountable to business hypotheses rather than screens. Second, trust is not a coat of paint. Sometimes the most important design decision is the name on the door.
