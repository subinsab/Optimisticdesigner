Case study · Aspero · Yubi · 2022 to 2024

The ₹860M turnaround.

A retail bond platform with ₹18 crore in losses, under 300 investors, and a product the company had quietly written off. Eighteen months of designing for trust rebuilt it into Yubi’s highest-growth revenue line.

01 · The business situation
A capable team, a real market, and a product nobody believed in.

Aspero had been live for over a year when I arrived. The market was real: retail bond investing was an underserved space in India. The team was capable. And the numbers were brutal: under 300 active investors, ₹18 crore in losses, and a product most internal stakeholders had quietly written off.

The deeper problem was not acquisition. First-time investors, people who had never bought anything beyond a fixed deposit, were landing on a platform that assumed they already knew what they were doing. Onboarding asked for information without explaining why. Listings showed data without explaining value. The brand communicated complexity when it needed to communicate calm. This was not a feature problem. It was a trust problem, and trust is a design problem.

02 · The strategy · The Trust Architecture
The diagnosis came before the design.
Retail investors don’t buy bonds. They buy certainty.

Most failing products get redesigned when what they need is re-thought. Before touching a single screen, I spent four weeks on diagnosis, structured around three questions: what is the product actually communicating right now, what do users need to feel before they will invest, and what is the gap between the two. Session recordings, user interviews, analytics deep-dives, and a full heuristic audit.

The answer reframed the whole engagement. We were not shipping features into a product. We were performing trust for people about to hand over their savings.

03 · The team, and how I ran it
A small senior pod, wired straight into the P&L.

I kept the team deliberately small: three designers, one researcher, and one content strategist, embedded with product and growth rather than sitting in a design silo. Content strategy was a first-class discipline from day one, because the language of trust matters as much as the interface of it.

We ran it like a business unit. Weekly reviews with the CEO, CFO, and CPO were about activation, repeat investment, and assets under management, never about mockups. Design owned the roadmap conversation, not just the backlog.

04 · The turnaround
Four moves, in order.
01Reposition the brandFrom complexity to calm. Every surface rewritten for a first-time investor, not a finance professional.
02Onboarding as a guided storyKYC came down from 11 steps to 5, and every remaining step explains why it exists and what the investor gets for it.
03Trust signals at decision momentsProof, protections, and plain language placed exactly where money changes hands, not in a help centre.
04Measure belief, not clicksActivation, repeat investment, and AUM became design’s scoreboard, reviewed with the C-suite weekly.
05 · Business outcome
The product nobody defended became the one everyone cited.
₹860MRevenue, from ₹18Cr in losses
20K+Active investors, from under 300
32%Share of Yubi’s total revenue
11 to 5KYC steps after the redesign

Eighteen months from the first diagnosis, Aspero reached positive ROI. The number that mattered most was not on any dashboard: the organisation believed in the product again, and put it at the centre of the growth story.

06 · What I carry forward
Products fail on belief before they fail on features.

Aspero taught me that trust is not a coat of paint applied to a product. It is the product. Every surface either builds belief or borrows against it, and a design leader’s job is to know which is happening on every screen.
