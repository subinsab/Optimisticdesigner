# The Optimistic Designer: Site Strategy v2

Goal: land the next Director of Design or VP of Design seat. Written 15 Jul 2026.

## 1. The one job of this website

A hiring executive or recruiter arrives here having just seen the resume or LinkedIn. In under 60 seconds they should conclude: "This person has run design as a business function at scale, twice, and is current on AI. We should talk." Every page, sentence, and number serves that conclusion. Anything that doesn't gets cut.

## 2. Audience, in priority order

1. The decider: CEO, CPO, or CPTO hiring a Director/VP of Design (fintech and SaaS, India, UK, Singapore, US, remote). They skim. They read numbers, titles, and the first line of every section.
2. The filter: executive recruiter doing a 3-minute credibility check against the resume. Consistency is everything for this reader.
3. The validator: a design leader on the interview panel checking craft and depth. They read one case study end to end.

The consulting buyer is deliberately demoted. Consulting appears once, framed as what Subin is doing between executive seats, and never competes with the primary ask.

## 3. Positioning

Narrative spine: "I've built the design function from scratch twice, turned it into revenue and P&L impact, and I run it at AI speed. I'm choosing my next Director or VP seat."

Three proof pillars, in the order a VP panel weighs them:

| Pillar | Claim | Receipts |
|---|---|---|
| Org builder | Built the function twice, and it outlasted me | 1 to 32 at HashedIn (HUX, acquired by Deloitte) · 2 to 20+ at Yubi across 13 products, 5 brands |
| Business operator | Design as a P&L line, not a service layer | Aspero ₹18Cr loss to ₹860M revenue · ₹72Cr FY24 + ₹186Cr FY25 ops reduction · 45% overhead cut |
| AI-scale leader | Ran the playbook before the market asked | Y.Design token-based multi-brand system, Figma-to-UI via Phoenix Codie and Figma MCP · consulting practice cutting design-to-dev cycles up to 40% |

## 6. Voice and style rules (locked)

Confident, warm, occasionally wry. Short declaratives ("I didn't break." "That conversation happened once."). No em-dashes, no arrows or symbol shorthand, middots allowed as separators. Numbers written as words in prose ("139 to 4"). Every line should read like Subin said it across a table.

## 8. What success looks like

Not traffic; interview conversion. A recruiter who reads resume then site finds zero contradictions and one new reason to advance. A hiring exec who reads only the home page can repeat the three pillars from memory. A design leader who reads the Aspero case concludes the craft is real.
